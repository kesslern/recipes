# Herb-Crusted Pork Loin Roast

## Why This Recipe Works
To get our herb crust to deliver maximum herb flavor in our pork roast recipe, we cut a single horizontal pocket across the middle of the roast that could hold a good quantity of herb paste. With our internal flavor guaranteed, we went to work on the crust, using the same herb paste we had used to fill the pocket. To keep it attached to the outside of the roast, we found we needed to score a crosshatch pattern into the fat cap, giving the paste something to grip.

## Before You Begin
- If only **“enhanced” pork** is available (the label will state that the pork was injected with a water-salt solution), do **not** brine the roast. Instead, simply season the stuffed and tied roast with salt before browning.
- Do not trim the pork of its layer of fat.
- While it is possible to substitute dried rosemary for fresh, do not substitute dried thyme for fresh or the herb crust will be dry and dusty tasting.
- Roasting time will vary widely depending on the thickness of the meat.
- The roast can be brined, stuffed, and tied a day ahead, but do not prepare the bread crumb topping until you are ready to cook.

## Time
2 hours, plus 1 hour brining

## Yield
Serves 4–6

## Ingredients
- 2½–3 pound boneless center-cut pork loin roast  
- Table salt  
- ¼ cup sugar  
- 1 large slice hearty white sandwich bread, torn into pieces  
- 1 ounce grated Parmesan or pecorino cheese (about ½ cup)  
- 1 medium shallot, minced (about 3 tablespoons)  
- 4 tablespoons olive oil, plus an additional 2 teaspoons  
- Ground black pepper  
- ⅓ cup packed fresh parsley or basil leaves  
- 2 tablespoons minced fresh thyme leaves  
- 1 teaspoon minced fresh rosemary or ½ teaspoon dried  
- 1 large clove garlic, minced or pressed (about 1½ teaspoons)

## Instructions
1. Lightly score the fat cap on the pork, making a ¼-inch crosshatch pattern.  
2. Cut a pocket horizontally through the center of the roast.  
3. Dissolve ½ cup salt and the sugar in 2 quarts of water in a large container. Submerge the roast, cover with plastic wrap, and refrigerate for 1 hour. Remove and pat dry thoroughly with paper towels.  
4. Adjust oven rack to the lower-middle position and heat oven to 325°F.  
5. Pulse bread in a food processor until coarsely ground, about sixteen 1-second pulses (you should have about 1 cup crumbs). Transfer crumbs to a medium bowl (do not wash the food processor workbowl). Add 2 tablespoons Parmesan, the shallot, 1 tablespoon oil, ⅛ teaspoon salt, and ⅛ teaspoon pepper. Toss with a fork until evenly coated.  
6. Add parsley or basil, thyme, rosemary, garlic, remaining 6 tablespoons Parmesan, 3 tablespoons oil, ⅛ teaspoon salt, and ⅛ teaspoon pepper to the empty food processor workbowl. Process until smooth, about twelve 1-second pulses. Transfer herb paste to a small bowl.  
7. Spread ¼ cup herb paste inside the pocket of the roast and tie securely. Season the roast with pepper (and salt if using enhanced pork).  
8. Heat remaining 2 teaspoons oil in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat until just smoking. Add roast, fat side down, and brown on all sides, 8–10 minutes, lowering heat if fat begins to smoke.  
9. Transfer roast to a wire rack set in a foil-lined rimmed baking sheet. Remove and discard twine.  
10. Spread remaining herb paste over the roast and top evenly with the bread crumb mixture.  
11. Roast until the thickest part registers 145°F on an instant-read thermometer, 50–75 minutes.  
12. Remove from oven and let rest for 10 minutes; internal temperature should rise to about 150°F.  
13. Transfer roast to a carving board, taking care not to squeeze juices from the pocket. Slice into ½-inch slices and serve immediately.
